# Skillfully RE5 learning-resource plan

**Edition status:** editorial blueprint and sample content, prepared 1 September 2026.
**Audience:** South African FAIS representatives preparing for RE5.
**Scope:** original explanatory learning material. It is not legal advice, an FSCA publication, or a substitute for the legislation and the learner's FSP procedures.

## 1. Editorial premise

RE5 is the Regulatory Examination for representatives in all categories of FSPs. The official **Preparation Guide: Regulatory Examinations (RE1 and RE5), 17 January 2026, Version 1** is the controlling exam map. It identifies eight RE5 tasks and their qualifying criteria (QCs). This textbook follows that map; it does not use commercial-provider notes, question banks or proprietary teaching structures.

**How the book speaks.** “Statutory anchor” boxes identify the instrument and section a learner must read. “In plain English” boxes are Skillfully's explanation, not statutory wording. Scenarios are invented teaching facts. A learner must always read the cited primary source after studying the explanation.

**Important:** a preparation guide tells us what is assessed; it does not freeze the law. The FSCA's legislation page itself warns that displayed Acts may not reflect later amendments. For that reason, no chapter should be released merely because it matches the guide: the currency controls in section 9 are mandatory.

## 4. Standard chapter template

Use this fixed template in every chapter so later legal updates are local rather than a rewrite of the book.

1. **Chapter promise and QC map** - task, QCs, K/S level, what a learner will be able to do.
2. **Source card** - instrument, sections, verified publication/commencement note, link, check date and legal-review status.
3. **The statutory anchor** - a short citation and a reading instruction. Use a short extract only when essential; never reproduce a lengthy provision.
4. **In plain English (Skillfully explains)** - clearly labelled explanation, with no quotation styling.
5. **The workflow** - a numbered decision path: facts to collect, disclosure/action, record, escalation.
6. **Worked original scenario** - invented names, no real client facts, source-tagged reasoning and a “what not to assume” line.
7. **Common exam traps** - definition boundaries, exceptions, timing and “most appropriate next step”.
8. **Knowledge check** - four original multiple-choice questions plus one short applied check; cite the QC each question tests.
9. **Answers and rationale** - explain why the best answer is best and why a tempting answer is incomplete; do not mimic an exam body's items.
10. **On-the-job checklist** - a short action aid marked “training aid - follow your FSP procedures”.
11. **Update hook** - source IDs, facts likely to change, owner and next review.

## 7. Knowledge-check design and original sample items

### Design rules

- Test one or more mapped QCs, never an invented syllabus point.
- Use four options, one best answer, and explain the source-based reasoning.
- Mark a question as `K` when it asks what the law/framework requires and `S` when the learner chooses the safest compliant action on stated facts.
- Do not copy or reverse-engineer past examination questions. Do not claim that a question will appear in RE5.
- Update answers whenever the source card changes; retain an item version and reviewer approval.

### Sample checks

1. **T8.4 (K):** A representative says, “I only completed the application, so FAIS cannot apply.” Which is the best response?
   - A. Correct; only recommendations are regulated.
   - B. Incorrect; an intermediary service may be regulated even where no advice was given.
   - C. Correct if the client signed the form.
   - D. Correct whenever the product supplier receives the form directly.

   **Answer: B.** The statutory definitions must be applied to the facts. Completion/bringing about a transaction can raise intermediary-service questions; a signature or route to the supplier does not settle that question. Read S02 s1 and S13.

2. **T4.2 (K):** A benefit offered by a supplier seems minor. What is the most appropriate first step?
   - A. Accept it because the amount is low.
   - B. Ask the client to approve it verbally.
   - C. Check it against the FSP's conflict process and the Code's defined rules before accepting or disclosing it.
   - D. Record it only after a sale.

   **Answer: C.** Everyday “minor” is not the legal test. Follow the Code and the FSP process. Read S03 definition and s3A.

3. **T5.2 (S):** A colleague needs the record of a recommendation to answer a complaint. The file cannot be located. What is the best action?
   - A. Recreate a note from memory and date it as the original.
   - B. Ignore the request because the client has not complained to the Ombud.
   - C. Escalate the missing record through the FSP's record-retrieval process, preserve what exists and accurately record the gap.
   - D. Ask the client to resend only the favourable messages.

   **Answer: C.** Retrieval and accurate records are controls. Backdating or selective reconstruction creates a further risk. Read S02 s18 and S03 ss3, 8-9.

4. **T8.11 (K):** A possible debarment issue is identified. Which statement is best?
   - A. A manager may announce debarment immediately without process.
   - B. The FSP should use the current formal process, including fairness and notification requirements, and keep the issue within authorised channels.
   - C. The representative should notify clients before the FSP decides.
   - D. Only the Ombud can deal with it.

   **Answer: B.** Debarment is regulated; exact steps and recourse must be checked against current law and guidance. Read S02 s14; S07 s153; S09; S13.

## 8. Sample chapter - Chapter 9: Being a representative: advice, intermediary services and safe boundaries

### What this chapter covers

**RE5 map:** T8.1 (roles and responsibilities, K), T8.2 (apply the role, S), T8.4 (distinguish advice and intermediary services, K).
**By the end:** you should be able to identify the facts that matter, avoid making a casual classification, and know the safe next step.

### Source card

- **Statutory anchors:** FAIS Act, definition of *advice*, *intermediary service* and *representative* in section 1; section 13. [S02](https://www.fsca.co.za/Information-Centre/)
- **Guidance anchor:** FSCA Guidance Note: Intermediary Services and Representatives. [S13](https://www.fsca.co.za/Regulatory%20Frameworks/Guidance%20Notes/GNIntermediateReps.pdf)
- **Exam-map anchor:** RE5 T8.1, T8.2 and T8.4. [S01](https://www.fsca.co.za/LR-FAIS-Fit-and-Proper/)
- **Checked for this draft:** 1 September 2026. **Publication status:** legal review required before release, because classification turns on full definitions and facts.

### Statutory anchor - what to read

Read the three section 1 definitions together, then read section 13. The Act distinguishes advice from intermediary services and sets the framework for a representative appointed by an authorised FSP. The guidance note helps with the factual boundary, including limited clerical, technical, administrative, legal or accounting work in a subsidiary or subordinate capacity.

This paragraph describes the sources. It is **not statutory wording**. Do not learn it as a substitute for the definitions.

### In plain English - Skillfully explains

Think of the client's journey in two parts.

1. **Advice** is about influencing a choice. If you recommend a particular product, suggest a course of action about a product, or guide a client towards one based on their position, you may be giving advice. The fact that the client asked for it, or that you call it “education”, does not decide the issue.
2. **Intermediary service** is about the product transaction or relationship being brought about, administered, maintained or serviced. Helping a client to put an already-made decision into effect may therefore still be a financial service even if you have not recommended a product.

Those are teaching shortcuts, not legal tests. The full statutory definitions contain inclusions and exclusions. The answer can change with the exact words used, the product, the authority given to you, the service performed, and whether your work involves judgement or leads a client to a particular transaction.

### A practical pause-and-check method

Before acting, use this five-question check:

1. **What did the client ask, and what did I say?** Capture exact words where the interaction moves beyond general information.
2. **Did I steer a choice?** A personalised recommendation or proposal is a warning sign for advice.
3. **Did I help bring about, maintain or service a transaction?** That raises an intermediary-service question.
4. **Am I appointed and authorised for this activity?** Do not rely on a job title or a supplier relationship. Check the FSP's authority, your appointment, licence conditions and supervision status.
5. **What does the FSP process require now?** Apply the relevant advice, disclosure, record and escalation steps. If classification is unclear, pause and escalate to the designated person; do not solve a legal boundary alone in front of the client.

### Worked scenario - Nandi's “quick recommendation”

Nandi calls Musa and says, “I have a new baby. Which of your life policies is best for me?” Musa asks about her budget and current cover, then says, “Take Plan A - it suits a young family like yours.” He starts the application on her behalf.

**Skillfully's analysis:** Musa's words are more than a neutral description of products. They point towards a particular product using facts about Nandi. That is a strong indicator that he may be giving advice. Starting the application may also bring the transaction about. The safe learning answer is not to debate labels; it is to follow the FSP's applicable advice and intermediary-service process, make required disclosures, keep the required record, and obtain help if he is not authorised or supervised for the activity. [FAIS Act s1 and s13 - S02](https://www.fsca.co.za/Information-Centre/); [Guidance Note - S13](https://www.fsca.co.za/Regulatory%20Frameworks/Guidance%20Notes/GNIntermediateReps.pdf)

**What we are not saying:** this invented scenario is not a final legal opinion on every fact pattern. It does not decide product suitability, disclosure content, competence, or a client's claim.

### Worked scenario - completing, without recommending

After comparing options herself, Zola says she has chosen Product Q. Priya reads the application questions aloud, enters Zola's answers and sends the completed form to the supplier. Priya does not suggest Product Q or compare it with alternatives.

**Skillfully's analysis:** absence of a recommendation does not automatically end the FAIS enquiry. The activity may still be intermediary service depending on the statutory definition and facts. Priya must not assume she is “only admin” without checking whether the limited exclusions actually apply. She should work within her FSP appointment and process, preserve an accurate record of what happened, and escalate a borderline case. [FAIS Act s1 and s13 - S02](https://www.fsca.co.za/Information-Centre/); [Guidance Note - S13](https://www.fsca.co.za/Regulatory%20Frameworks/Guidance%20Notes/GNIntermediateReps.pdf)

### Common traps

- “The client chose first, so FAIS cannot apply.” Not necessarily; intermediary services can still matter.
- “I gave facts, so it was definitely not advice.” Facts can become guidance when combined with a personalised proposal or recommendation. Apply the definition.
- “My employer calls me a consultant, so I am authorised.” A job title does not replace appointment, authority and current fit-and-proper/supervision requirements.
- “If it is urgent, I can sort the paperwork first.” Urgency does not remove the need for the correct process, disclosure and record.

### Knowledge check

**1. T8.4 (K).** A client asks for the features of two products. The representative describes the stated features accurately and does not recommend either product. What is the best answer?

- A. It can never be a financial service.
- B. It is definitely advice because products were mentioned.
- C. More facts are needed; apply the statutory definitions and the actual interaction.
- D. It is advice only if the client buys a product.

**Best answer: C.** Product discussion alone does not settle classification. Read the complete definitions and consider what was actually said and done. `Source: S02 s1.`

**2. T8.2 (S).** You are unsure whether a client's instruction has moved from factual information to a recommendation. What is the best next step?

- A. Continue, but call the discussion “general information”.
- B. Pause, capture the facts and use the FSP's escalation/process route before proceeding.
- C. Ask the client to waive all rights in writing.
- D. Send the client directly to the product supplier and delete your notes.

**Best answer: B.** A label or waiver does not settle a statutory classification. Accurate notes and a controlled escalation protect the client, the representative and the FSP. `Source: S02 s1, s13; S13.`

### On-the-job checklist

Training aid - follow your FSP procedures.

- Record the client's request and your response accurately.
- Check your appointment, authority, product scope and supervision status.
- Use the right advice/intermediary-service workflow; make no promises that the source or FSP process does not support.
- Give required disclosures through the required channel and retain evidence.
- Escalate unclear classification, authority or competence questions promptly.

### Legal content requiring qualified review before publication

1. Verify the current consolidated text and every exclusion in the FAIS definitions.
2. Verify appointment, licence-condition and supervision propositions against the specific FSP category and current notices.
3. Review the factual language in both scenarios to ensure it does not accidentally state a universal legal conclusion.
4. Cross-check cross-references to the current General Code, Fit and Proper Requirements and exemptions before adding detailed advice-process or disclosure lists.

## 9. Editorial update and legal-change process

### Trigger and triage

| Trigger | Owner | First action | Release effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| New FSCA RE guide or mapping | Managing editor | Compare each task/QC and legislation reference line by line | Freeze affected chapters until the map and assessments are remapped. |
| Act, regulation, Code, notice, rule or exemption change | Legal reviewer | Confirm gazette/official instrument, commencement, transitional provisions, repeal/savings and audience impact | Red/amber/green impact label; no “green” legal claim without sign-off. |
| FSCA guidance, interpretation or form changes | Regulatory editor | Identify whether it changes teaching explanation, workflow, form, URL or only context | Update scenario/process cards; preserve the legal source hierarchy. |
| Ombud Council rules or FIC framework change | Specialist reviewer | Check complaint/AML content and assessments | Withdraw affected answer keys if a result can change. |
| Annual scheduled review | Source librarian | Re-fetch every S01-S15 link and record version/hash/retrieval date | Issue an edition change log even where no substantive change is found. |

### Required workflow

1. **Detect:** subscribe to FSCA notices/communications, Ombud Council publications and official gazette notices; perform monthly source checks and a full quarterly review.
2. **Capture:** save the primary instrument, stable URL, publication date, effective/commencement date, provision affected and retrieval date in the source register.
3. **Interpret:** a qualified South African financial-services legal reviewer records the plain-language proposition, limits, transitional treatment and approval. The writer does not infer commencement from a publication date.
4. **Impact-map:** search the manuscript, glossary, examples, knowledge checks, answer keys, downloads and marketing claims by source ID and section.
5. **Revise:** update the source card first, then explanatory text. Highlight changed learner-facing content in the edition log. Do not silently overwrite a rule that changes an answer.
6. **QA:** a regulatory editor checks every citation; a subject-matter reviewer tests every updated knowledge check; legal reviewer signs off high-risk chapters.
7. **Release:** publish edition number, “law checked to” date, a concise change log and a notice that learners should use current primary material. Archive the prior edition and its source pack.

### Publication stop rules

Do not publish or sell an affected chapter if any of the following is unresolved:

- validity/commencement of an exemption, notice, form or rule;
- a repeal, savings or transitional provision (including the Long-term Insurance Act reference);
- a debarment timeframe, procedure, notification or recourse statement;
- an AML customer-due-diligence, reporting or confidentiality statement;
- a complaints jurisdiction, compensation limit, time limit or Ombud procedure statement;
- a claim that an activity is definitely advice, not advice, an intermediary service, or outside FAIS on incomplete facts.
